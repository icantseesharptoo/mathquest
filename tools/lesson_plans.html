Lesson plan — Angles: lines, points & triangles

Duration45 minutes (demo lesson). Extend to 60 min by giving more time to the handout and the Gacha game.
Curriculum linkCambridge Lower Secondary Mathematics 7, Learner’s Book Unit 5 “Angles and constructions”: 5.1 A sum of 360°, 5.2 Intersecting lines, 5.3 Drawing lines and quadrilaterals. Builds toward IGCSE 0580 C5.5 (angles at a point, on a line, vertically opposite, triangle & quadrilateral sums).
Learning objectiveUse angle facts to calculate unknown angles on a straight line, at a point, between intersecting lines, in triangles and in quadrilaterals.
Success criteriaI can name acute / right / obtuse / reflex angles · use 180° on a line and 360° at a point · use vertically opposite angles · use 180° (triangle) and 360° (quadrilateral) · write a short calculation to justify my answer · (challenge) form and solve a simple equation from angle facts.
Prior knowledgeTypes of angle; simple addition/subtraction; (for challenge) collecting like terms.
Key vocabulary (EN — RU)angle — угол · vertex — вершина · straight line — прямая · vertically opposite — вертикальные углы · sum — сумма · triangle — треугольник · quadrilateral — четырёхугольник · isosceles — равнобедренный · protractor — транспортир
ResourcesSlides: Y7_Angles_Lesson_Slides.pptx (teacher notes in each slide) · Handouts: Y7_Angles_ClassHandout_A/B.pdf (alternate versions to neighbours) · Answer keys (TEACHER_…) · MathQuest web app (Year 7) on projector · mini-whiteboards, protractors, rulers · paper triangles for the tear-the-corners demo.

Differentiation & support

  • Support: Level 1 only, “Remember” box and Russian glossary; partner talk before writing; diagrams drawn to scale so students can sanity-check answers.
  • Core: Levels 1–2, show every calculation.
  • Challenge: Level 3 (angles with algebra), measure-&-draw challenge, invent a puzzle for a partner.

Common misconceptions

  • Mixing up 180° and 360° → ask “is this a straight line or a full turn?”.
  • Trusting the picture: triangles/quadrilaterals are “not drawn accurately”.
  • Treating adjacent angles on a line as vertically opposite.
  • Reading the wrong protractor scale (inner/outer).

Assessment & homework

AfL: mini-whiteboards, self-marked handout score, exit ticket. Homework: Y7_Angles_Homework.pdf (answer key separate), or generate a unique sheet per student in MathQuest → PDF Worksheets (use each student’s register number as the “seed”). Optional: students earn coins by practising in MathQuest.

Lesson plan — Solving linear equations

Duration45 minutes (demo lesson). Slides 9–10 (fractions, negative answers) can be taught as extension or in the next lesson.
Curriculum linkCambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 (2025–2027) C2.5 Equations: “Solve linear equations in one unknown” (e.g. 3x + 4 = 10; 5 − 2x = 3(x + 7)). Learner’s resource: Grade 10 Mathematics learner book (linear equations chapter).
Learning objectiveSolve linear equations, including brackets, fractions and the unknown on both sides, and check solutions by substitution.
Success criteriaI can undo operations with inverse operations · keep an equation balanced · expand brackets and clear fractions · collect x-terms when x is on both sides · check my answer by substituting · (challenge) form an equation from a word problem.
Prior knowledgeDirected numbers, expanding a single bracket, order of operations, simple fractions.
Key vocabulary (EN — RU)equation — уравнение · solve — решить · expand — раскрыть скобки · inverse operation — обратная операция · term — слагаемое · coefficient — коэффициент · substitute — подставить · fraction — дробь
ResourcesSlides: Y10_LinearEquations_Lesson_Slides.pptx · Handouts: Y10_LinearEquations_ClassHandout_A/B.pdf + keys · MathQuest web app (Year 10: Learn → Guess & check, Gacha) · mini-whiteboards.

Lesson flow

TimeActivityTeacherStudents
0–4Starter — Think of a number (slide 3)Pose “double it, add 3, get 13”. Ask how they found it → “working backwards”.Solve mentally, explain method to a partner.
4–10Balance & inverse operations (slides 4–5)Balance picture; golden rule. MathQuest → Learn (Year 10) → Guess & check: type wrong x values and watch the sides stay unbalanced.Predict “heavier side”; complete the inverse table.
10–20Worked examples 1–3 — two-step, x on both sides, brackets (slides 6–8)Reveal one line at a time (MathQuest Next step button also works with fresh random numbers). Think aloud; always check by substitution.After each example, try a similar one on whiteboards.
20–24Quick-fire boards (slides 11–12)4 equations of increasing difficulty; “3-2-1 show me”.Show answers; fix errors.
24–29Gacha game (slide 15)Summon a Problem Capsule (rarity = difficulty). Reveal worked solution step by step. Show Prize Capsule with demo coins.Teams race on whiteboards.
29–41Handout (slide 16)Circulate; insist on one step per line with = signs aligned. Fast finishers: Level 3 + Error hunt.Individual work; self-mark with key.
41–45Plenary + exit ticket (slides 13, 17)“Spot Sam’s mistake” bracket error; collect exit tickets (2x + 1 = 9).Explain error; confidence rating.

If short of time: skip slides 9–10 (fractions, negatives) and the real-life slide; the handout’s Level 3 then becomes homework.

Differentiation & support

  • Support: Level 1 + 2 with “Remember” box; keep to one-step-at-a-time “Next step” reveal; use the guess-and-check balance to build meaning; Russian glossary.
  • Core: Levels 1–2; check every answer by substitution.
  • Challenge: Level 3 (brackets with subtraction, cross-multiplying, decimal/fraction answers, perimeter modelling), Error hunt, “create your own equation with x = 5”.

Common misconceptions

  • Operating on only one term (e.g. subtracting 5 from one side only).
  • Partial expansion: 3(x + 4) → 3x + 4; sign errors with −(x + d).
  • Moving a term without changing its sign.
  • Treating x as a label rather than a number; not checking.

Assessment & homework

AfL: whiteboards, handout self-score, exit ticket. Homework: Y10_LinearEquations_Homework.pdf (+ teacher key), or unique worksheets from MathQuest (seed = register number). Optional: 10 problems in MathQuest Practice (Hard) to earn a Prize Capsule.
